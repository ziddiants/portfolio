problem: >-
  Children aged 5–17 meet general-purpose AI unsupervised, in copy-the-answer mode, while their thinking habits form. Neither the child nor the parent can tell whether any understanding happened.
insight: >-
  We interviewed 6 children with their parents, and working professionals. Adults who struggle with AI are the symptom; schooling never built the thinking layer. One parent put the product in a sentence: teach him AI, but don't let AI think for him.
decision: >-
  No open chat anywhere. The child attempts first, and the system explains only afterwards. Answers that can be marked in code are; AI judges only written answers, against a rubric. The trade-off: no homework help, the job most children already use AI for.
mvp: >-
  Guardian-created accounts; a placement check under ten minutes; lessons for three age bands; 55 lessons across 4 subjects; mastery counted only when a correct answer comes with an explanation or a transfer; every AI call screened, with alerts to parents.
measure: >-
  Verified mastery events per active learner, per week.
